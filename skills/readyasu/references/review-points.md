# Reading the check output, and reviewing by eye

Read this when you look at lint findings in SKILL.md Step 2, item 1, and when you check the findings of `readyasu_check.py run` in Step 4. When Python cannot run, follow section 4.

Findings are places to check against the original (the material, when composing), not orders to change. Do not keep rephrasing only to make findings disappear. Revise once only, and run again after the revision (SKILL.md Step 4). Having no findings does not prove that the meaning was kept. Lint gives no score; do not treat the number of findings as a pass mark.

## 1. The first lines of the output

- Line 1 says what to do next. Follow it.
- Line 2 is the status line (it starts with `readyasu-check:`). Copy it into the check record. When you ran verify before submitting, copy the second line of the verify output instead.
- "Checks run:" summarizes what ran; it corresponds to "Checks run" in the check record.
- "Stiffness check:" and "Energy check:" give values such as "none", "candidates 2" or "reference 1". Copy them to the lines of the same name in the check record. "reference" means the doc type normally changes these things (stiffness in incident, formal and paper; energy in pr, article, incident, manual, formal and paper); still read the section. When these lines are absent (compose mode), leave them out of the record.
- "Review findings: original N → final M" counts lint rule matches before and after. It is not a score, and a rise or fall says nothing about readability. Copy the values to "Review findings" in the check record.
- "Flagged words kept:" lists stock vocabulary, metaphorical verbs, jargon and project words that are still in the final draft and were in the original. Reread each sentence with the Step 2 questions (SKILL.md Step 5).
- "Flagged words added by the rewrite:" lists such words that your rewrite introduced. Restore the original wording. In compose mode, words found in the material count as kept and the rest as added.

## 2. Sections of the output

### == Changed places ==

A summary of the first ten changes, shortened to about 120 characters. Look here first for changes you did not intend; check shortened places and the eleventh onward in "Full diff". When a place says "(N lines → M lines)", different paragraphs may be paired; read the pairs around it.

### == Meaning risks ==

Each item is tagged with its kind. Counted kinds make `--fail-on meaning-risk` fail; "[not counted]" kinds are listed for review only. Compare each with your note from before rewriting (SKILL.md "Top rule: keep the meaning"). Restore unintended changes. Keep a change based on the material only when it meets fact-recovery.md section 3, and report it under "Fact check".

| Kind | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| number | A number was removed, added or changed: digits, number words, quarters and versions (Q3, v2), currency and magnitudes ($1.5M, 5k), percentages; a unit written next to an unchanged number (30 seconds → 30 ms, 30 days → 30 business days); a month or weekday name; a statistic word next to unchanged numbers (median → mean, p95 → p50); a frequency (daily → hourly, billed monthly → billed annually; "every night" equals "nightly"). The whole-text line catches numbers that disappeared from or appeared in the whole text | Restore, unless it is a fill or correction from a source (fact-recovery.md), an absolute date you added after a relative one, or a count word you removed with a count announcement or a cleft list ("Three things:") while every item stayed |
| approximation | about, up to, at least, more than... on the same number changed | Restore the original approximation |
| negation | The number of negations (not, no, never, n't, without, unless...) changed in the paired sentences; or the count stayed the same but the kind changed ("does not restart" → "restarts without delay"); or what is denied swapped sides ("the replicas, not the primary" → "the primary, not the replicas"). A negation that replaces "silently" or "quietly" ("no alert fired"), and removing "not just" or "not only" ("not only faster but also cheaper" → "faster and cheaper"), are listed as not counted; check that what "not just X" affirmed is still there | Read both. Removing "not only" or turning "not X but Y" into "Y" also changes the count; that is fine only when the rephrase test passes |
| certainty | A hedge (may, might, could, would, likely, seems, appears to/that, looks like, it's possible, unclear whether, as far as we know, we assume, hopefully, is believed to, we expect, planned...) disappeared or was replaced by a stronger one, also inside the answer of a folded self-answered question. Stacked hedges ("could potentially", "can possibly", "it seems that ... might") count as one, so reducing a stack to one hedge is not reported | Restore the hedge at the same strength |
| booster | always, guaranteed, definitely, ensures... was added. Counted only for pr, article, incident, proposal, manual, formal and paper (not when the original sentence already has a strong obligation word such as must or required); otherwise listed as a review finding | Remove the added certainty |
| permission | you may, allowed to, optional, no need to... appeared or disappeared | Restore; permission is not ability ("may" is not "can") |
| obligation | should ↔ must, or an obligation word appeared or disappeared. A negated one ("don't have to", "not required") is not an obligation, and "You must restart" → "Restart" (an imperative starting with the same verb) is not reported; in formal documents, where "must" is a defined term, keep it (diff does not flag this) | Restore the original strength |
| status | TBD, pending, under investigation, not yet, unconfirmed... disappeared | Restore the state; do not turn it into a finding or a plan |
| hearsay | reportedly, according to, per the vendor, says, said, told us, claims, noted, mentioned, colleagues say, users report... appeared or disappeared | Restore; hearsay is not assertion |
| question | A question became a statement or the reverse. A short self-answered question folded into a statement, and a request turned into a polite question, are listed as not counted | Restore the sentence type, except when you folded a self-answered question into a statement (SKILL.md "Decoration and ad copy") or turned a request into a polite question for a stated relationship (register.md section 7) |
| request | please, could you... disappeared | Restore the request form; a bare imperative changes politeness |
| scope | A limiting word (only, solely) disappeared; either/or ↔ both/all; or a determiner such as some, most, many or several disappeared with no other partial word, or a partial word became all/every, or one quantifier became another (some → most), or all/every/each now stands where a restricting word stood ("Annual subscriptions" → "All subscriptions") | Restore the scope |
| condition | A condition, time limit or qualifier disappeared: if, unless, until, when, while, once, before, after, as soon as, as long as, provided, except where/as/for, at its (sole) discretion, at launch, initially, for now, temporarily; or a qualifier phrase that opened the sentence ("Under normal traffic,", "During peak hours,") whose words are gone | Restore it; without it the sentence is true in more cases. A rewrite that keeps the condition in other words is fine; a concessive "while" rewritten with "but" or "although" is not reported |
| correlation | A cause word was added to a sentence that stated only a correlation | Restore the correlation wording |
| commitment | we will, we guarantee, will never happen again... was added | Remove it unless the request or material states the commitment |
| citation | A citation ([1], (Smith, 2020)) changed | Restore; which source supports which claim must not move |
| name_order | The same names appear in a different order, or a name moved to another sentence or line (owners swapped between list items) | Check who does what to whom; restore if the roles swapped |
| name | A name left a sentence and appears nowhere in the final draft (the name that replaced it, if any, is shown: "Dana asked Lee to review it" → "Dana reviewed it"), or one name was replaced by another in the same sentence ("approved by Dana" → "approved by Lee") | Restore the name and who does what |
| material_number | (compose) A number in the final draft is not in the material | Remove it or use the material's value |
| contrast_reversed [not counted] | A word the original denied ("not X", "rather than X"; not "not just X", which affirms X) now appears with focus, priority, first, both, and... | If X moved to the affirmative side, the claim flipped; restore the negation (rephrase-test.md section 3). A rewrite such as "First, reduce X" can also appear; read which side X is on |
| code | A value or name inside inline code, a code block or a URL changed (`retention_days = 30` → `retention_days = 7`) | Restore it exactly unless the original or material gives the new value |
| protected [not counted] | Inline code, URLs, e-mail addresses or code blocks added or removed | Restore them exactly when the change was not intended |

Some forms are not caught. Compare sentences without findings against your note too.

### == Review findings from the diff ==

| Kind | What to check |
|---|---|
| certainty_weakened | The rewrite is less certain than the original. Restore unless the original overstated something a source corrects |
| hedge_changed | A hedge was replaced by another of the same level. Usually fine; check the nuance |
| ability | can / able to disappeared from a sentence about a person ("You can export"). Check that the sentence still says what is possible. With a non-person subject, "can corrupt" → "corrupts" is a counted certainty risk |
| request_added | A request marker appeared. Check that the sentence was a request (register.md) |
| quantifier_gone | An adverb such as often, usually or mostly disappeared without a replacement. Check that the sentence did not become always true |
| limiter_added | only, solely... appeared. Do not add limits the original lacks |
| condition_added | if, unless, until, at launch... appeared. Do not add conditions the original lacks |
| causal_added | because, led to, as a result... appeared. Keep facts side by side if the original did not connect them |
| booster | Certainty word added (in doc types where it is not counted). Remove guarantees the original lacks |
| voice | Active/passive changed with the same names. Check who acts |
| quote | Text in quotation marks changed or disappeared. Restore dialogue, quoted words and labels |
| quote_changed | Text inside a block quotation (`>`) changed. Block quotations are compared sentence by sentence like the body, so their meaning risks are counted too. Restore quotations; callouts such as `> **Note:**` or `> [!WARNING]` are the writer's own text and may be revised |
| feeling_added | A feeling word appeared that the original lacks. Do not add feelings, even in essays |
| connection | A rewritten sentence starts with a connective or "this/it". Say what it connects to; fix or ask |
| actor_added | A role (team, manager, we, you...) or a name appeared that is in neither the original nor the material. Remove invented actors, owners and recipients |
| deleted | A deleted sentence held a negation, hedge, obligation, permission, condition, limit (only), hearsay or status word. Check that nothing appears only there, and that its content does not come back elsewhere with a different negation or hedge |
| added | An added sentence holds a negation, obligation or permission. Check that the original or material says so |
| words_gone | Content words that no longer appear anywhere. Check that no fact went with them |
| heading | A heading changed or the number of headings changed, or a closing section at the end ("Conclusion", "Final thoughts"; not in pr or minutes, whose headings stay) was removed. Check the warnings, conditions and roles it held; a closing section may go only when it repeated the body |
| stance | With `--stance`: "let's" in a rule document, "will" added in an explanation, or an obligation added to a recommendation |

### == Stiffness ==

Contractions dropped by more than half, or stiff words (utilize, facilitate, prior to, in accordance with...) appeared. Unless the request asked for a more formal register, restore the original's register. In incident, formal and paper this is reference only.

### == Energy ==

Exclamation marks, emoji or headings decreased. Check that the writer's energy was not lost where the document type keeps it (promo, mail, essay voice), and that headings kept their number and order. In pr, article, incident, manual, formal and paper this is reference only; for other specified types, emoji and heading items are reference only (except promo and mail for emoji, and promo, minutes and mail for headings).

### == Material match ==

Shown when material was passed (`--material`, or material sections on standard input).
- "Not in the material (numbers)" and "(names)": values in the final draft found in neither the material nor the original. In compose mode, numbers here are counted meaning risks.
- "Number found, but its unit, month or weekday is not in the material": the number is there, but with a different unit or date word ("22 seconds" where the material says "22 minutes"). Treat it like a number not in the material. It also lists a number whose material line has no unit word (`RETRY_INTERVAL_SECONDS = 0.5`); keep such a fill when the excerpt line has the same value and claim (fact-recovery.md section 2, step 6), and check that the line's name matches the sentence (`retry_wait = 3` is not "3 retries"). When you filled a value, the excerpt must have the line it came from (fact-recovery.md section 2, step 6).
- "Values on changed lines that match a material line": only lines you changed are listed (up to 15; the rest are counted); lines marked "(original)" come from the original, not the material. It shows only that a line has the same value. It does not prove the line is the source or that the value is right.
- "Hedge or status words in the material that the final draft lacks": check that the final draft kept "planned", "not yet", "may" and the like where it uses those values.

### == Fact gaps ==

With `--fact-gaps`: sentences of the final draft that refer to a value they do not give (reference: "before the deadline", "as discussed"), or give only a metaphor (figure), an effect (effect), a degree word (degree) or a change without values (change). The list is partial and not counted. Sentences with a number, a hedge or plan word, a vague quantity or a closing resolution are never listed. Fill only when a source has the content (fact-recovery.md); otherwise ask under "Fact check" or leave it (for [degree] items, ask only in reports whose readers act on that value; [reference] items are worth asking about when readers must act). In formal and paper, do not fill from the conversation or files; list such content under "Facts that could be filled" (fact-recovery.md section 6). Not shown for essay and promo.

### Attempts

The first line counts attempts and revisions. Rerunning on an unchanged final draft (for example when verify did not match) is not a revision. A run continues the previous one only when the original (in compose mode, the material) is the same, the working folder is the same, the session ID (`--session` or `READYASU_SESSION`) is the same (a run with a session ID and one without never continue each other), and the previous run was less than two hours ago; otherwise a new run starts at attempt 1. For a new request about the same text, add `--new-run` to its first run only (SKILL.md Step 3).

### == Lint findings on the final draft ==

The lint findings of the final draft; see section 3 for each rule.

### == Full diff ==

Every changed line, not shortened. A one-line change is shown as one `~` line with word-level changes as `[-removed-]{+added+}`; other changes show the removed (`-`) and added (`+`) lines. The same text is in `attempt-N.diff.txt` next to the record.

## 3. Lint rules

In the document title (the first level-1 heading), whose words are not changed, only `emoji` and `bold_unrendered` are reported. `readyasu_lint.py` prints each finding as `L<line>:<column>`, with the line cut around the match.

| Rule | Severity | How to look at it |
|---|---|---|
| `bold_unrendered` | error | Markdown will show the asterisks. Fix the markup: no space inside `**text**`; add a space after a closing `**` that follows punctuation and precedes a letter (`**Note:** This`). A lone `**` (the only one in the paragraph, as in `**kwargs`) is info: add the closing `**` if bold was meant, or put code in backticks. `**` between letters or digits (`2**3`) is not reported |
| `emoji` | warn | Remove decoration; keep emoji that carry meaning (a status icon in a table the reader needs). A flag, a keycap, a skin-tone or ZWJ sequence (👨‍👩‍👧) is one finding. Symbols that are emoji only with the emoji selector U+FE0F (ℹ️, ▶️, ‼️, ➡️, ©️) are reported only with it; arrows, notes, stars and check marks that are not emoji (→, ➜, ♪, ★, ✓) are not reported. Reported in the document title too. Exempt in promo and mail |
| `em_dash` | warn | Replace clause-splicing dashes with punctuation that keeps the scope; split into two sentences when a reason or condition would reach farther. Keep in fiction narration and dialogue, quotations, ranges and code. Not reported in the document title (the first level-1 heading), for a dash that is a whole table cell, between two range endpoints (`2019 – 2021`, `Monday – Friday`, `9 am – 5 pm`, `$5 – $10`, `v1.2 – v1.4`, `January – March`), after a time, date, version or numbered label (or a range of them) that starts any line or item (`09:12 — Config change`, `10:00–10:15 — Status`, `9–10 am — Standup`, `2025/12/03 — Kickoff`, `Version 2.1 — Effective January 1`, `1.2.0 — Added dedup`, `v1.0–v1.4 — End of life`, `2.0.0 – 2.3.1 — Legacy branch`, `Step 1 — Install`, `Part II —`, `Option A —`, `Slide 1 —`, `Section 3 —`, `Session 2 —`, `Episode 12 —`, `Act II —`, `2026-11-13 (Thu) — Miniprep`), after any other capitalized word and a number or roman numeral that start a line when no sentence ends after the dash (`## Milestone 2 — Beta`, `SEGMENT 2 — Interview`; not `Python 3.9 – the oldest we support – is end of life in October.`, and not after a preposition or quantifier, `Over 50 — and counting`), after an all-capitals label of up to four words that starts a line when no sentence ends after the dash (a stage direction: `**[COLD OPEN — ambient sound: wind, birds]**`, `[THEME MUSIC — 10 sec, then under]`; not `[UPDATE — the fix is now live for all customers.]`), after inline code or a URL that is all that comes before the dash on its line, also in bold, as link text or as aliases joined by a comma, a slash or "or", optionally with placeholders in capitals or angle brackets after it (an option list: `` `--threads N` — number of workers ``, `` `-h`, `--help` — show help ``, `` `-l`, `--level` LEVEL — show only ... ``, `` `--out` <path> — ... ``; em dash, en dash and `--` alike; a link with words, `[Guide](guide.md) — ...`, is still checked, and so is a word in capitals with no code or URL before it, `(FYI — the deploy moved to 5pm.)`, `_IMPORTANT_ — back up first.`), on the paragraph under a man page's `NAME` heading when only names come before the dash (`logsift — filter log files`), before a date, a due time or an owner that ends a list item (`- Grace: confirm the quote — Fri 8 Mar`, `— next meeting`, `— before HR meeting`, `- Fix the alert — Marco`) or a title line (a heading, or the first line of a paragraph when no sentence ends before the dash, no period follows and the next line does not go on in lower case: `Platform team weekly — Tue 5 Mar 2024, 10:00–10:45`, `## Sprint review — Fri 8 Mar`), for a dash used as a bullet in plain text (a line that starts with the dash after a line ending with a colon or next to a line that starts with the same dash: `Decisions:` then `– Budget approved`), before a field label (`— Owner: Marco`), or on a line that only names the author under a quotation (`— Jeff Atwood`). The message quotes the words around the dash |
| `chat_residue` | warn | Assistant chat left in a document: delete. In mail, courtesies, offers and closings (`closing-courtesies.txt`, including "I hope this helps") are not reported; the writer's own sign-off stays. Interjections such as "Sure!" and "Of course!" (`chat-interjections.txt`) are reported only where they start a sentence, and not in essay, script and mail, or inside double quotation marks. "Thank you for your attention" is not reported in slides and script (the closing slide or line). An assistant's preamble before a drafted or revised text is found by a pattern, at the start of a sentence and not after a greeting line (`Hi Ana,` then "Here's a draft reply you can send to the customer:" is the writer's mail): after an interjection when the clause ends with a colon or the clause is in the first paragraph and that paragraph ends there or with a colon ("Certainly! Here's a revised version:", "Sure! Here's a draft email you can send:", "Sure — here's a draft you can send to your landlord. It's polite but firm:"), reported in every doc type; without one, only in the first paragraph: a revision lead-in that ends with a colon, with a revision word (revised, polished, updated, simplified, condensed, corrected, reworked, a comparative such as shorter, friendlier or more formal) and a text noun ("Here's a polished version of your email:", "Here's your revised email:", "Here's the corrected text:") or "Here's a draft:" (in mail only when a mail follows), or with any other noun when a greeting or `Subject:` line follows ("Here's the updated invitation:" then "Dear guests,"); an offer such as "Here's a reply you can send to Jordan:" when a greeting, quotation, code block or `Subject:` line follows; and a mail noun without a verb offered as a draft (after "a", "an" or "your", or with possible, suggested, proposed, draft or revised: "Here's a draft email to your landlord:", "Here's a possible response:", "Here's the suggested reply:"; not "Here's the email:") when a greeting or `Subject:` line follows. The offer and the mail noun are reported in every doc type. A thematic break (`---`) between the preamble and the mail is skipped. Without a verb, a mail someone wrote or sent is never reported, also after an interjection: a noun followed by "from ..." or by a clause with its own subject and a past-tense verb ("Here's a quick note from our CEO:", "Here is the letter my grandfather wrote in 1944:", "Here's the reply I got from Stripe support:", "Here's the email that went out to customers on Monday:", "Sure, here are the notes from Tuesday's call:"). After the mail's sign-off (`Best,` then a name) or right after a thematic break, an offer that speaks about the drafted mail (its tone, wording or formality, a shorter or another version, "adjust it", "make it more ...") is reported in mail too ("Let me know if you'd like me to adjust the tone or make it more concise!"), and there offers that no word list has are found in every doc type ("Feel free to adjust the dates or tone.", "Would you like a shorter version for SMS as well?"). Lead-ins in documentation and the writer's own sentences are not reported ("Here are the options you can use with `tinyq worker`:", "Here are the updated numbers:" before a table, "Here is the email template you can copy for outage notices:", "Here's a sample email:", "Here is the revised version of the runbook with your comments addressed.", "Here's a draft implementation of the retry logic.") |
| `stock_phrase` | warn | Openers, signposts and closers that announce instead of saying. Delete, and keep any fact that appears only there. Besides `stock-phrases.txt`, any "In today's ... world/landscape/age/era/market" opener is reported when only modifiers come between "today's" and the noun and the noun ends the phrase ("In today's rapidly evolving healthcare landscape,"; not "In today's call we reviewed the business plan") |
| `hedge_stack` | warn | Use one hedge of the same strength. Never remove the hedge entirely. After "it seems" or "it appears", "could" counts only as a possibility (`could be`, `could have`), not as an ability (`the attacker could read any file`). A modal and adverb that stress a question, a negation or a comparison are not reported ("What could possibly go wrong?", "no way we could possibly finish", "as fast as you can possibly go"). Hedges that wrap to the next line are found |
| `heading_colon` | warn | Remove the colon from the heading (also inside emphasis, as in `## **Benefits:**`). A heading that ends with code or a link after the colon (`` ## Option: `--force` ``) is not reported, nor is the document title |
| `bold_label_list` | warn | SKILL.md "Bold and labels": keep labels for values, fold summary labels into the sentence, remove the bold. The message says "Item" for list items and "Paragraph" for paragraphs. Turns of a dialogue or an interview are not reported: a run whose labels are all speaker names (one to three capitalized words or initials, with an optional note in parentheses: `**G:**`, `**OWEN:**`, `**Q:**`, `**MAYA (V.O.):**`) and repeat (`**G:**`, `**O:**`, `**G:**`). In script, a run of speaker-name labels is not reported even without a repeat (character names in the script's format). Exempt in minutes and slides |
| `heavy_bold` | warn | Keep bold only for what readers must not miss. A bold label at the start of a line does not count, on every line of the paragraph (a metadata header: `**Status:** Final`, `**Author:** Sam`, `**Severity:** SEV-2`) |
| `ai_vocabulary` | info | slop-catalog.md section 3 and section 10. Keep the field's terms. "Key takeaways" is not reported in slides (a summary slide is normal there) |
| `wordy_phrase` | info | slop-catalog.md section 7. Use the short form when meaning and register stay the same |
| `metaphor_verb` | info | slop-catalog.md section 1. Say what changed, using only what the original or material says; otherwise keep the verb and ask. Do not replace it with a vague benefit. Not exempted in any type; each doc-type file says which uses to keep |
| `intensifier` | info | SKILL.md "Wordiness, zombie nouns and stock vocabulary": delete when stacked, on an absolute, or in technical and formal text |
| `jargon` | info | domains/business.md section 3 |
| `negative_contrast` | info | SKILL.md "Self-labeling and negative contrast" and rephrase-test.md section 3. Keep negations that correct a likely misreading. A concession (`not tested, but it shipped`, `not a major one, but customers noticed it`) and a negation inside a condition, time or concession clause (`If the value is not set, it is read from the environment`, `Although the API is not stable, it is widely used`) are not reported; after "but" a noun phrase must follow (an article, a possessive, "about", "rather", or one word that ends the clause) and end the clause soon or go on with a preposition or "that" (`not a blocker, but the team should review it`, `but the default timeout went from 30s to 10s` and `not a perfect fix, but the patch works` are concessions). "Not just X" affirms X: when removing the contrast, keep X. In the one-sentence form the subject is a pronoun, a noun phrase, a name (`React isn't a framework; it's a library.`) or one to three words that start the sentence (`Remote work isn't a perk; it's a paradigm shift.`, also with a dash), and both subjects must agree (`He wasn't the one who called; she was.` is not reported). The two-sentence form needs the same subject and verb (`It isn't a tool. It's a mindset.`, `We didn't build a tool. We built a process.`, `We didn't just ship features. We shipped value.`); the first subject is a pronoun or one to three words that start the sentence (`Remote work isn't a perk. It's a paradigm shift.`, `Ledgerly Flow isn't just a tool. It's ...`), also after emphasis, two spaces, a colon or a callout label (`**This isn't a tool. It's a mindset.**`, `> **Note:** It isn't a bug. It's a feature.`), but not an imperative (`Please don't use the old endpoint. It uses a deprecated auth flow.`), and a chain is found at its last pair (`This isn't a tool. It's not a framework. It's a mindset.`). Neither form is reported for a status or place (`I'm not sure. I'm checking.`, `We're not ready. We're still waiting on QA.`, `I'm not in the office. I'm at the dentist.`, `The API isn't stable yet; it's still in beta.`, `Your subscription isn't affected. It's active until May.`), when the second part adds with "also" (`It isn't cheap. It's also slow.`), for "it's" and a past participle (`This isn't the first time. It's happened twice this quarter.`, `This isn't a regression. It's been broken since 2.3.`), or for a dummy "it" before an adjective and a to-, that- or wh-clause (`It isn't clear what caused the spike.`, `It is not possible to delete a project. It is possible to archive it.`), unless "just", "only", "merely" or "simply" follows the negation in the one-sentence form. Also checked in headings with an article or "just" after "not" (`## Not just a linter, but a formatter`; not `## Not now, but soon`) and across wrapped lines, but not in the document title. The form "What we needed was not X but Y" (SKILL.md "Two-sentence negative contrast") is still reported; give that as the reason under "AI-like wording kept". The semicolon join of a denied verb that SKILL.md recommends for a two-sentence contrast (`We didn't need a new tool; we needed a shared way to use the old one.`) is not reported; with "is", the join is the one-sentence form (`It isn't a tool; it's a mindset.`) and is reported |
| `personification` | info | SKILL.md "Metaphorical verbs and personification". Keep obvious functions and identifier subjects in developer documents. Includes a tool that "chose to" or "decided to" do something |
| `self_answered_question` | info | Fold into a statement. Reported for short questions answered right after (`The result? Fewer errors.`, `Why does this matter?`, and up to nine words for `But what does this mean for developers?`), and for a question of up to ten words followed by "Simply put", "In short" or "The answer". Exempt in slides, promo and script |
| `fragment_run` | info | Write subject and verb when the original gives them. Inline code and URLs count as words, and an instruction with an object (`Open Settings.`, ``Run `apt update`.``) is not a fragment; a lone `Build.` is. Exempt in minutes, slides, essay, promo and script |
| `count_announcement` | info | Check that the count matches the items; if not, ask without changing either. Exempt in slides |
| `closing_summary` | info | Delete a closing section that only repeats; keep new information, next steps and requested summaries. Exempt in slides |
| `excess_list` | info | Turn reasons and conditions back into sentences where a list splits one argument; keep parallel lists. Exempt in pr, incident, minutes, manual and slides |
| `exclamation` | info | Keep only where the type and writer use them. Also found before an emoji (`We shipped v2! 🎉`). Exempt in essay, promo, mail and script |
| `sentence_start_repeat` | info | Vary only where it reads as a pattern; keep parallel steps. Numbered clauses (`3.1`, `3.2`) do not count |
| `decorative_colon` | info | Remove the colon or finish the sentence. Not reported when code, a URL or an e-mail address follows the colon on the line (`` run: `pip install x` ``), for a link reference definition (`[g]: https://...`), for a letter's salutation on its own line (`Dear Ms. Alvarez:`, `To whom it may concern:`, `To the Graduate Admissions Committee:`, `To the editor:`), or before paragraphs that start like lettered or numbered items (`(a)`, `a)`, `(iv)`, `(1)`) |
| `project_term` | info | A word from `.readyasu-words/flag.txt` or `flag-patterns.txt`. Rewrite the sentence, not just the word; the replacement after "→" is the project's preferred term |

## 4. Reviewing by eye (when Python cannot run)

Write `readyasu-check: not_run reason=no_python` in the check record and write "Checks not run: lint (no_python), diff (no_python), final match (no_python)" (add "material match (no_python)" when material was given). Then, before submitting:

1. Compare every sentence of the final draft with the original for numbers, negations, hedges, permission and obligation words, status words, hearsay, questions, requests, limits (only), names and their order, citations, code and URLs.
2. Search the final draft for the lint rules in section 3: emoji, dashes, bold labels, stock phrases, chat residue, stacked hedges, headings with colons.
3. Check that you added no actors, feelings, causes, commitments, guarantees, evaluations or recommendations.
4. Do not write that the checks were run.
