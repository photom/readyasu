# Writer and reader (register)

## 1. When to read this

Read this only when the request states who writes to whom ("an email to a client", "a message to my manager", "a notice from the city to residents"). Without that, do not change politeness, directness or the writer's subject, and do not mention register in your answer.

## 2. Three values

- Writer: the role of the writer (individual, team, company, public body).
- Reader: the role of the reader (peer, manager, client, customer, public).
- Setting: what the text is for (request, apology, notice, report).

Write each value as (specified) or (inferred) in the check record; infer only the setting, and only when the text makes it obvious.

## 3. How to decide

Use what the request says. Do not infer a relationship from names or titles in the text alone.

## 4. What may change on a specified relationship

| Relationship | Adjust | Keep |
|---|---|---|
| To a manager or senior colleague | Requests become polite questions ("Could you review this by Friday?"); remove commands ("Review this by Friday.") | The deadline, the request, the content |
| To a client or customer | "We" for the company's acts; polite requests ("Please let us know", "Would you be able to"); apologies the original makes stay at the same strength | Commitments: do not add "we will" or "we guarantee" that the original lacks |
| To the public (notice) | Neutral, impersonal where the original is; plain words | Legal wording and defined terms |
| To a peer | Contractions and direct requests are fine if the original uses them | Strength of requests |

## 5. Who

Add "we" or "I" only when the request says who the writer is. Do not turn a system action into the company's act ("the system sends a reminder" stays).

## 6. To whom

Use the reader's name or role only as the original gives it. Do not add a greeting or sign-off that the original lacks, except in mail when the request asks for a complete message.

## 7. Matching politeness

- Raise politeness by changing the form of the request, not by adding hedges that weaken it: "Send the file." → "Could you send the file?" keeps it a request; "If you get a chance, maybe send the file" weakens it.
- Lower politeness only when asked.
- Do not add apologies, thanks or flattery the original does not have.

## 8. Notes by setting

- Apology: keep what the writer apologizes for; do not add promises that it will not happen again.
- Refusal: keep the refusal clear; do not turn "we cannot" into "we may not be able to".
- Request with a deadline: keep the deadline in the same sentence as the request.

## 9. How to report

Write the values in the check record. Report register changes under "What changed" in one line ("raised requests to polite questions for a client").
