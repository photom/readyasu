# Writer and reader (register)

Details of the fourth item of SKILL.md section 2, Step 1: the writer's role and the relationship to the reader. Whatever the values, keep the meaning (numbers, negations, conditions, certainty, who did what), do not add content the original lacks (owners, explanations, claims, feelings, greetings, apologies), and keep the structure. All examples are synthetic.

## 1. When to read this

- When the request states the writer ("I'm writing as the team lead", "from our support team") or the reader ("for a client", "to my manager", "for all employees"). Read this before fixing the body. Also read it when unsure whether to ask about the relationship.
- When nothing is stated, write inferred values in one line of the check record and change neither the body nor your notices. Do not mention politeness or register.

## 2. Three values

- Writer: the role, in the request's words, and how they speak (an individual / on behalf of a team / for an organization). Do not invent a role that was not stated.
- What the writer can know: an individual knows their own work and what they saw or heard; a team lead knows their team's work, decisions, goals and numbers, and decisions of meetings they attend; an organization's voice knows what the organization decided and announced. Phrases such as "I heard from", "at the stand-up", "according to the newsletter" or "I'm told" show a channel; when one is in the original or request, do not treat the content as something the writer could not know.
- Reader: who (free words), the relationship (one of the seven below), and the level (expert, informed, general; audience.md section 7).
- Setting: the document type (one of the thirteen in SKILL.md section 2, Step 1) or "not decided".

| Relationship | Examples | Default level |
|---|---|---|
| Manager or senior | manager, director, advisor | informed |
| Same team | colleague, reviewer | expert |
| Junior | new hire, successor | informed |
| Another department | finance, another team | informed |
| Everyone inside | all employees, the whole department | all employees: general; the department: informed |
| A specific outside party | a client, a vendor's contact | from their words (customers: general; a vendor's contact: informed) |
| Everyone outside | all customers, readers of a public post | general; for technical articles and papers, do not decide |

Use the default level only when the relationship was stated, and write "informed (inferred from the relationship)". A stated level takes priority.

## 3. How to decide

Use what the request says. Do not infer a relationship from names, titles or greetings in the text alone.

## 4. When nothing is stated

Keep the original's politeness, contractions, "we" or "I", and directness. Do not mention that you left them alone, and do not add a "register notice".

## 5. Who

Add "we" or "I" only when the request says who the writer is. Do not turn a system action into the company's act ("the system sends a reminder" stays). Do not make the writer claim knowledge outside what they can know (section 2); if the original does, keep it and ask.

## 6. To whom

Use the reader's name or role only as the original gives it. Do not add a greeting or sign-off that the original lacks, except in mail when the request asks for a complete message.

## 7. Matching politeness

| Relationship | Adjust | Keep |
|---|---|---|
| To a manager or senior | Requests become polite questions ("Could you review this by Friday?"); remove bare commands ("Review this by Friday.") | The deadline, the request, the content |
| To a client or customer | Polite requests ("Please let us know", "Would you be able to"); "we" for the company's acts when the writer speaks for the company | Commitments: do not add "we will" or "we guarantee"; apologies at the same strength |
| To everyone outside (notice) | Neutral wording, plain words | Legal wording and defined terms |
| To a peer or junior | Contractions and direct requests are fine if the original uses them | Strength of requests |

- Raise politeness by changing the form of the request, not by adding hedges that weaken it: "Send the file." → "Could you send the file?" keeps it a request; "If you get a chance, maybe send the file" weakens it.
- Lower politeness only when asked.
- Do not add apologies, thanks or flattery the original does not have.
- When the original mixes registers (contractions in one paragraph, none in the next) without intent, align them to the one that fits the relationship.

## 8. Notes by purpose

- Apology: keep what the writer apologizes for; do not add promises that it will not happen again.
- Refusal: keep the refusal clear; do not turn "we cannot" into "we may not be able to".
- Request with a deadline: keep the deadline in the same sentence as the request.

## 9. How to report

- Write the three values in the check record ("Writer: support team (specified) / Reader: client, informed (inferred from the relationship) / Setting: mail (inferred)").
- Report register changes under "What changed" in one line ("made requests polite questions for a client").
- A "register notice" is a line under "Questions for the writer" that says something the writer must decide because of the relationship (for example, an internal name the outside reader will not know). Like "Needs an explanation", it does not count toward the three questions.
