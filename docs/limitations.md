# Limitations

## What the checks can and cannot do

- The scripts match fixed patterns. They report places to review; they do not decide whether meaning was kept or whether the text is easy to read. A run with no findings does not prove anything about meaning.
- Lint counts are not a score. A rise or fall in "Review findings" says nothing about readability.
- The record from `readyasu_check.py` shows that the checks ran on a final draft with a given hash. Anyone who can edit both the draft and the record can change them; the record helps find skipped runs, it does not prevent tampering or prove human approval.

## Known gaps in lint

- Word lists match surface forms. A listed word used in its literal or technical sense ("unlock the door", "robust statistics", "elevate the platform by 2 meters") is still reported; use `.readyasu-words/allow.txt` or keep the word and give the reason.
- Word lists, project words and the pattern rules see words inside underscore emphasis (`_seamless_`, `__leverage__`), but not a word inside a snake_case name that is not in backticks (`enable_seamless_mode`).
- Words mentioned as examples inside quotation marks (a style guide that says to avoid "delve") are reported like any other use. Only interjections such as "Sure!" are treated as dialogue inside double quotation marks (or with `--doc-type` essay, script or mail); dialogue in single quotation marks is reported.
- Rhythm-level AI patterns (every paragraph the same length, a "rule of three" in every sentence, a summary sentence at the end of every paragraph) are not detected.
- Negative contrast, personification and self-answered questions are detected only in common forms. "Not X, but Y" is reported only with an article or "about" after "not" (in a heading also "just", "only", "merely" or "simply", so "## Not supported, but planned" is not reported) and, after "but", a noun phrase that starts with an article, a possessive, "about" or "rather", or a single word that ends the clause ("not a bug, but intended behavior" is missed). That noun phrase must end the clause within about four words or go on with a preposition, "that", "which" or a pronoun ("but a platform for building products"); a verb after it makes the sentence a concession, which is not reported ("not a blocker, but the team should review it", "but the default timeout went from 30s to 10s", "but the tests pass"). The verb test is a guess from word shapes (modals, a short list of irregular past forms, -ed words, a word after a plural), so a real contrast with such a word is missed ("but a feature requested by users", "but the sales team"). A negation in a clause that opens with if, when, although, because, since, while, once, until, as and similar words is not reported, so a real contrast inside such a clause is missed too. A capitalized word before the negation counts as the subject ("React isn't a framework; it's a library."), so a sentence that starts with any capitalized word is checked the same way. The two-sentence form is reported only when both sentences have the same subject (a pronoun, a noun phrase or a name, followed by "it" or "they") and verb (including common irregular and doubled past forms: "build" and "built", "stop" and "stopped"), and not when either sentence states a status or a place (sure, ready, done, available; in, on, at, off), goes on with an -ing form, or the second goes on with "still" or "not" ("It's not a bug. It's working as designed." and "It isn't in the cloud. It's on your laptop." are missed). Personification is checked only for software and document subjects (the tool, the system, the code, the logic); "the agent" and "the assistant" are left out because they are often people.
- Interjections ("Sure!", "Great question") are reported only at the start of a block, a table cell, a sentence or a line that starts with a capital, so one that follows a comma is missed.
- Sentence splitting uses punctuation and a short list of abbreviations; unusual abbreviations can split a sentence in two. Words that are also abbreviations before a number (no, max, fig, Dec) end a sentence unless a digit follows, and a single capital letter is treated as an initial only before a capitalized word that does not usually start a sentence ("John F. Kennedy", but "Plan B. It was cheaper."), so "Plan B. Kennedy approved it." stays one sentence. An abbreviation right after a number ("450 ms.", "5 p.m.") ends a sentence only before a capitalized word that usually starts one ("Meet at 5 p.m. The room is booked." is two sentences, "at 5 p.m. Smith joined" one). Emoji right after the end mark stay with the sentence when a new sentence or the end follows ("We shipped v2! 🎉" then "Thanks to everyone."), so the exclamation mark is still found. Inline code or a URL after sentence-ending punctuation counts as a capitalized word and starts a new sentence ("The fix? `retry()` now wraps every call." is two sentences), also after an unlisted abbreviation.
- The em_dash rule skips timeline entries, fields, ranges and attributions by pattern: a time, date, version or numbered label at the start of any line or item, alone or as a range ("09:12", "Dec 5", "2025-12-03", "2025/12/03", "03.12.2025", "Day 2", "Step 1", "Part II", "Option A", "10:00–10:15", "9–10 am", "Dec 3–5", "Version 2.1", "1.2.0", "Last updated: March 2026"; also on a hard-wrapped line, so "We met at" then "10:00 — it was short." is missed), a spaced dash between two range endpoints (numbers with a currency sign, %, am/pm or a unit of up to five letters, versions, Q1–Q4, capitalized month and weekday names), inline code or a URL (also in bold or as link text) that is all that comes before the dash on its line, alone or as aliases joined by a comma, a slash or "or" (an option list: "`-h`, `--help` — show help"), a dash that starts a line in plain text after a line that ends with a colon or next to a line that starts with the same dash (bullets in plain-text minutes or mail), a capitalized label of up to three words with a colon after the dash ("— Owner: Marco"), and a line that is only a dash and a capitalized name after a sentence ("— Jeff Atwood", "— Plato, Republic"). So a clause dash between two such tokens ("We grew 20% – 30 new customers signed") is missed, and other separators are still reported.
- fragment_run does not count a sentence that starts with a common instruction verb and has a word after it ("Open Settings.", "Click Save."), so a run of short slogans that start with such a verb ("Sign up now. Start free.") is missed.
- hedge_stack skips a modal and adverb after a question word that opens the clause or a negation or "as ... as" among the four words before it ("What could possibly go wrong?"); a real stacked hedge in such a clause is missed.
- Markdown parsing covers headings (ATX and setext), lists (a line indented at least to an item's text belongs to the item, even after a blank line or a fence; as in CommonMark, a new list interrupts a paragraph only with a bullet or "1.", so a wrapped line that starts with "1998." continues the paragraph, also inside a list item, while a sibling item ("2." after "1.") still starts an item; inside an item, a fence indented four or more columns past the item's text after a blank line is indented code), block quotes (with lazy continuation lines and fenced code inside), tables, fenced and indented code, HTML comments and YAML or TOML front matter (only with a key or comment on line 2 and a closing line). A block quote indented four or more spaces inside a list item is checked as text. Deeply nested structures and raw HTML blocks may be classified roughly: a line that starts with a block-level tag (`<div>`, `<p>`, `<details>`) or holds a single tag is skipped, but text on the lines between `<div>` and `</div>` is checked.

## Known gaps in diff

- Sentences are paired by similarity. A deleted sentence and an added sentence that are similar enough are compared as a moved sentence, and half of a split sentence is joined to its neighbor. When a rewrite reorders or merges many sentences, a change may still be reported against a neighboring sentence, or a number may be listed as removed in one place and added in another.
- Negation, hedges, permission, obligation, conditions and scope are compared by counting marker words in paired sentences. Changes expressed without those words (an antonym such as "succeeded" → "failed", a verb that changes direction, "increase" → "decrease") are not caught. Read every changed sentence.
- "Names in a different order", names moved between sentences or lines, and names gone or replaced use capitalized words. A word at the start of a sentence counts as a name for these checks only when it is also capitalized in the middle of some sentence (for added actors: when it is followed by a verb and does not appear in lower case anywhere). A capitalized word that appears in lower case in the final draft ("Peak latency" folded to "peak latency") is not treated as a gone name. Swapped roles described with common nouns ("the client asked the vendor") are not caught.
- "over" and "under" before a number are read as approximations, also when they are prepositions ("spread over 3 regions").
- Headings are not used for the "name" check, because they are often in Title Case.
- Units are compared only when written right after the number (one qualifier word may sit between: "30 business days"). Months and weekdays are compared by name; dates written only as numbers are compared as numbers.
- "One" is treated as a pronoun, not the number 1, only in "no one", "one another", and after "the", "this", "old", "new" and similar words at the end of a phrase or before "who", "that" or "which" ("the old one."). A count written that way ("keep the one.") is not compared.
- Added actors are found from a list of role words and capitalized names.
- Fact gaps are a partial list of figures, effects, degree words and changes without values.

## What has been tested

- The scripts are tested with pytest (unit tests for the text handling, every lint rule, every meaning-risk kind, check run and verify, and the consistency of the documents with the scripts) on Linux with Python 3.13. CI runs the tests on Python 3.9 and 3.14 on Linux and 3.12 on Windows.
- The rewriting procedure (SKILL.md) has not yet been measured by running a model on a test set, and installs through the plugin, skills CLI and GitHub CLI have not been tried.
